# Catalyst Research

## Source hierarchy

Prefer, in order:

1. SEC filings and official exchange notices.
2. Company investor-relations releases and earnings materials.
3. Government or regulator publications.
4. Direct transcripts or official presentations.
5. High-quality financial news.
6. Secondary aggregators.
7. Social media only as a lead to verify elsewhere, never as primary proof.

## Verification checklist

- Confirm the exact issuer and ticker.
- Record event date and publication timestamp.
- Distinguish a new event from an old story being recirculated.
- Verify at least two credible sources when practical.
- Determine whether the catalyst is scheduled, completed, rumored, delayed, or denied.
- Check whether price has already moved substantially.
- Identify the next date or fact that can invalidate the thesis.
- Build the strongest bear case.
- Never use analyst price targets as the primary thesis.

## Common catalyst traps

- Earnings beat with weak forward guidance.
- “Partnership” without disclosed economics.
- Preliminary, nonbinding, or conditional agreements.
- Regulatory milestones that are not approvals.
- Buyback authorization confused with actual purchases.
- Old filing dates surfaced as new.
- Stock splits mistaken for value creation.
- Low-float hype and coordinated promotion.
